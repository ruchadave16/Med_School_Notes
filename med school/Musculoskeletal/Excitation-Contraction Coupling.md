*Mechanism that converts muscle action potential to tension*

$Ca^{2+}$ initiates process
- [[Tropomyosin]] normally blocks binding sites for myosin on actin 
- When Troponin C binds to some of this Ca2+, contraction gets activated

![[Screenshot 2026-04-29 at 2.34.57 PM.png]]

>[!Note]
>[[Skeletal muscle]]: excitation causes Ca2+ ↑ so troponin C saturation and max crossbridges
>	Each twitch at full power
>[[Cardiac muscle]]: excitation only causes some of crossbridges to form
>	Increasing Ca2+ increases force of heartbeat
>
1) Cross-bridge cycle
	- Initially, no [[ATP]] bound to myosin and myosin bound tightly to actin
	- ATP binds, causing change in myosin that releases it from actin
	- [[Myosin]] is displaced to plus end of [[actin]] (flexion) and attaches further away
		- ATP hydrolyzed and ADP is still attached to myosin
		- Energy released is used for power stroke to cock the head back
	- Eventually it loses structure and the filaments slide
	- ADP released and myosin becomes rigid again
	- Cycle continues **while calcium** bound to troponin-C (for skeletal)
	![[Screenshot 2026-04-29 at 1.22.09 PM.png]]
	![[Screenshot 2026-04-29 at 1.09.54 PM.png]]

2) If calcium reaccumulated by [[Sarcoplasmic Reticulum|sarcoplasmic reticulum]] Ca/ATPase, intracellular calcium decreases and calcium released from troponin-C
	- Tropomyosin blocks myosin from binding with actin
	- If reaccumulation doesn’t happen and muscle is stimulated repeatedly, then intracellular Ca remains high → sustained contraction called [[Clostridium Tetani|tetanus]]

>[!Clinical]
>[[Myotonic Dystrophy]] → inability to relax muscle at will
>Due to excessive calcium intake so contractions last longer and muscles don’t relax
>
>[[Malignant Hyperthermia]]
>
>[[Poliomyelitis]]
