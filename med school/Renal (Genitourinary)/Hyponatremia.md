*Definition: Na < 135 mEq/L*

#### Key Causes
- Loss of free water
	- [[Diarrhea]]
	- [[Vomiting]]
	- [[Burns]]
- Sodium loss
	- [[Mineralocorticoids|mineralocorticoid]] deficiency so ↓ aldosterone, ↓ Na reabsorption
	- Family history or personal history of [[Bartter syndrome]] or [[Gitelman syndrome]]
	- Brain injury
- ↑ solute
	- [[Hyperglycemia]]
	- [[Mannitol]] administration to treat [[cerebral edema]]
	- ↑ protein (like [[multiple myeloma]])
- ↑ Free water
	- [[Pancreatitis]]
	- [[Heart failure]]
	- [[Liver failure]]
	- [[Kidney failure]]
- Meds
	- [[SIADH]]

#### Physical Exam
- Skin turgor poor = hypovolemic
- Low extremity edema = hypervolemic
- JVP elevated = hypervolemic
- Orthostatic BP = hypovolemic
	- Systolic blood pressure drops by 20mmHg or more
	- Diastolic blood pressure drops by 10mmHg or more
	- Heart rate increases by 30 beats per minute or more
- Cap refill > 3 sec = hypovolemic

#### Lab Values
- Sodium level
- Serum osmolality (SOsm) 
	- > 300 = hypertonic hyponatremia due to [[mannitol]], [[hyperglycemia]], [[IV IG]]
		- **TREATMENT: per increase of BG by 100, correct by increasing Na ~2 points**
	- If ~300, isotonic hyponatremia
		- [[hyperlipidemia]], [[hyperproteinemia]]
	- If low, get UOsm (hypotonic hyponatremia)
### Differential
#### Volume Status
- Hypervolemia
	- [[Congestive Heart Failure|CHF]]
	- [[cirrhosis]]
	- [[Nephrotic syndrome]]
	- [[Kidney failure]] 
- Euvolemia
	- [[SIADH]]
	- Endocrinopathies: [[Addison Disease]], [[Hypothyroidism]]
	- ↑ water intake
	- Exogenous ADH
- Hypovolemia
	- Cerebral salt wasting syndrome -> following brain injury
	- Mineralocorticoid deficiency
	- Salt losing nephropathy
	- [[Burns]]
	- GI losses
	- Type 4 renal tubular acidosis
#### Urine Osmolarity
- Urine osmolality < 100 mOsm/kg
	*Here, ADH is turned off, or absent. Body is compensating by losing water in urine*
	- Euvolemic, low salt intake
	- Psychogenic/ primary polydipsia (↑ water intake)
	- Beer potomania / Tea and toast diet 
	- Endurance exercise
	- ##### Treatment = ↑ salt intake and ↓ water intake
- UOSm > 100 mOsm/kg 
	- *Here, ADH is present so check urine sodium next*
	- Urine Na < 20 mEq/L = ~↑ [[aldosterone]]
		- *Body is trying to compensate by absorbing more Na+ and losing K+ from urine*
		- If hypervolemic, [[heart failure]], [[liver failure]], or [[nephrotic syndrome]]
			- ##### Treatment = [[Diuretics|diuretics]]
		- If hypovolemic, GI loss, Third space losses ([[pancreatitis]], [[hypoalbuminemia]]), burns, sweating
			- ##### Treatment = volume expansion by giving isotonic fluids
	- Urine Na > 20 mEq/L = inappropriately low aldosterone
		- If hypovolemic, could be 
			- Because of diuretics
				- ##### Treatment = stop diuretics
			- Or could be salt wasting syndromes
				- [[Bartter syndrome]]
				- [[Gitelman syndrome]]
				- [[Cerebral salt wasting]]
				- ##### Treatment = ↑ salt intake through oral sodium tablets IV normal or hypertonic saline
			- [[Osmotic diuresis]] (glucose or mannitol)
				- ##### Treatment = fix hyperglycemia, IV normal or hypertonic saline
			- Mineralocorticoid deficiency
				- ##### Treatment = [[Fludrocortisone|fludrocortisone]]
		- If isovolemic
			- [[SIADH]]
				- ##### Treatment = stop drug causing it and monitor or use normal saline
			- [[Desmopressin]] use -> stop the med
			- Adrenal or glucocorticoid insufficiency
				- [[Addison Disease]]
				- [[Tuberculosis|TB]]
				- adrenal hemorrhage
				- Sudden stop of [[Synthetic Corticosteroids|synthetic corticosteroids]]
				- ##### Treatment = [[hydrocortisone]]
			- [[Hypothyroidism]]
				- ##### Treatment = thyroid hormone replacement
		- Hypervolemic
			- Renal failure -> [[dialysis]]
### Correction Rate
- Acute, severely symptomatic hyponatremia: 1 mEq/L/hr, until you have corrected 4-6 mEq/L (~6 hours)
	- Do NOT exceed 8-10 mEq/L for acute hyponatremia
- Serum sodium concentration is <120 mEq/L or if the patient has chronic hyponatremia: no more than 6-8 mEq/L in a 24 hour period
