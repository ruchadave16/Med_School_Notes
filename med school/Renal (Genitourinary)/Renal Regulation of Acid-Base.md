Kidneys secrete  $H^+$ from [[tubular cells]] to lumen of [[nephron]] and reabsorb $HCO_3^-$  

1) Resorption of $HCO3-$ 
	- Most happens in [[Proximal Tubular Transport]], rest in thick ascending limb
	- Bicarb buffer dissociation: H+ secreted through Na+ H+ exchange pump (Na+ reabsorbed)
	- HCO3- through cotransport with Na+ (reabosrbed into blood)![[Screenshot 2026-08-31 at 10.55.13 PM.png]]
	- Presence of **carbonic anhydrase** in tubular lumen keeps the rxn focused on breaking down so H2CO3 does not build up
		- pH remains relatively normal here because H2CO3 is being broken down so H+ is being continuously used up. This means H+ concentration remains ~ constant and the Na+ H+ pump does not face a huge gradient

	- In the collecting duct, this is different: 
	- Here, H+ secretion is through H+ ATPase pump and NO carbonic anhydrase is present in the lumen
		- tubular pH can significantly decrease here
		![[Screenshot 2026-08-31 at 11.08.31 PM.png]]
	
	- HCO3- is also secreted slightly in the collecting duct area by $\beta$ cells
		- This doesn't generally play a role
		- In [[Alkalosis|alkalosis]], secretion and ↓ reabsorption could be related to kidney's response 
		![[Screenshot 2026-08-31 at 11.13.01 PM.png]]

2) Titratable Acid Excretion
	- Proximal Tubule: Secreted H+ is combined with weak acid (amount of base needed to titrate back to 7.4 (neutral) measures the mEq of the H+ excreted this way)
	- Phosphate buffer: More effective buffer in tubular fluid than in plasma![[Screenshot 2026-08-31 at 11.18.49 PM.png]]

	- Collecting duct: 
	![[Screenshot 2026-08-31 at 11.23.15 PM.png]]

	- This mechanism accounts for excretion of 10-30 mEq H+ per day and can increase by a lot during [[acidosis]]

3) Ammonium Excretion
	1) NH3 generation and secretion in proximal tubules
		- Take up amino acids that produce NH4+ (secreted into tubule) in exchange for Na+
		- Process forms HCO3- which is absorbed (NEW)
		![[Screenshot 2026-08-31 at 11.35.47 PM.png]]
	
	2) NH4+ reabsorption by the thick ascending limb of Henle
		- Secreted NH4+ reaches thick ascending limb
		- Co-transport of Na+, NH4+, and 2 Cl- into cell and also by itself
		- Dissociation into NH3 and H+ 
		![[Screenshot 2026-08-31 at 11.37.16 PM.png]]

	3) NH3 secretion in collecting duct
		- Reacts with H+ that is secreted to make NH4+ (trapped and excreted)
		- This can ↑↑ in [[acidosis]]

**NH4+ excretion mechanism is the most important means for increasing H +
excretion during an acidosis**

4) H+ Secretion Control
	- ↓ pH in cell means ↑ H+ transport in luminal membrane  and ↑ glutamine uptake and deamination
	- ↑ PCO2 means more rxn with H2O and more production of cell H+ causing ↑ secretion
	- Na+ reabsorption increases luminal negative difference so ↑ H+ secretion
	- ↓ plasma K+ means K+ leaves and H+ enters cells
	- Aldosterone: direct effect on H+ - ATPase in collecting duct
