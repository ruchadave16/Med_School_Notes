### Etiology
- Most common in women 20-30 yrs
- Complicates 1-2% pregnancies

### Pathophysiology
- Inflammation of kidneys caused by bacteria or other micro-organisms
- Patchy interstitial suppurative inflammation
- Intratubular [[Neutrophils|neutrophil]] aggregates
- Tubular injury

Additional problems
- Papillary necrosis
	- Distal two-thirds of the pyramids have areas of gray-white to yellow necrosis
	- [[Coagulative Necrosis|coagulative necrosis]]
- Pyonephrosis
	- Suppurative exudate is unable to drain
- Perinephric abscess
- Emphysematous pyelonephritis 
	- Gas production in renal tissue
	- Seen almost only w diabetic patients uncontrolled
### Clinical Features
- Costovertebral pain
- Fever, rigors, chills, nausea, vomiting
- +- UTI symptoms

### Diagnosis
- Urine culture before antibiotic treatment
- Peripheral leukocyte count
- CRP to monitor therapy response
- Serum creatinine

- Imaging not indicated 
### Treatment
- Hospitalize if unstability, worried abt abscess
- **IF vomiting**, single parenteral dose of ceftriaxone, 1 g, or of gentamicin, 120 mg
	- follow with oral therapy
- Empiric parenteral therapy
	- Third-generation cephalosporins (use in pregnancy) -- [[ceftriaxone]]
	- [[Aminoglycosides]]
	- [[Ciprofloxacin]]
- Then oral therapy by culture
	- [[Ciprofloxacin]] most common
	- Nitrofurantoin, pivmecillinam, and fosfomycin should NOT be used for pyelonephritis

### Complications
- ↑ morbidity -- hospitalization for 20% of non-pregnant women
