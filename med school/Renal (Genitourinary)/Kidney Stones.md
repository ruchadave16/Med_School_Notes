### Crystals
*Form by precipitation of urinary salts when their solubility is affected by pH,
temperature, and concentration*

1) Uric Acid Crystals
	- No significance when transient
	- If ↑↑, 
		- Increased nucleoprotein turnover (chemotherapy)
		- [[Lesch-Nyhan Disease]]
		- [[Gout]]
	- Precipitate in concentrated, slightly acidic urine
	
	![[Screenshot 2026-09-15 at 12.38.03 AM.png]]

2) Calcium Oxalate Crystals
	- Small, colorless octahedron like an envelope
	- If ↑↑, 
		- Severe chronic renal disease
		- ethylene glycol or methoxyflurane toxicity
		- [[Crohn's Disease|crohn disease]] or ↑ absorption of oxalates
		
![[Screenshot 2026-09-15 at 12.39.40 AM.png]]

3) Ammonium Magnesium Phosphate Crystals
	- "Coffin lid" shape
	- Seen in infected urine, especially [[Proteus]]
	- Cause massive calculus, alkaline urine
	![[Screenshot 2026-09-15 at 12.40.56 AM.png]]

4) Ammonium Biurate Crystals
	- Brown, spiky bodies
	- Seen in infected urine

![[Screenshot 2026-09-15 at 12.41.48 AM.png]]

5) Amino Acid Crystals
	- Rare, but abnormal
	- Cystine crystals
		- Colorless, refractile, hexagonal plates – see in acidic urine
		- Pt has [[Cystinuria]] or Cystine calcui
	- Tyrosine crystals
	- Pt with [[liver failure]] or [[tyrosinosis]]
	![[Screenshot 2026-09-15 at 12.43.28 AM.png]]
