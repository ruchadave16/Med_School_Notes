### Etiology
- 7% lifetime risk 
- Most common in adolescents and young adults

### Pathophysiology
- Sudden inflammation of appendix 
- 50% to 80% cases are associated with obstruction of the appendiceal lumen
- Neutrophilic infiltration of the muscularis propria
	- neutrophils will infiltrate serosa causing a fibrinopurulent reaction = Serositis
### Clinical Features
- Symptoms
	- Pain starts at T10 because appendix = midgut 
	- As peritoneum involved, pain migrates to RLQ (McBurney’s point)
- Nausea, vomiting
- Anorexia
- Signs
	- Rebound tenderness – pressure to anterior abdominal wall stretches parietal peritoneum, ↑↑ localized pain when released
	- Rovsing sign = pain in the right lower quadrant with palpation of the left side
	- Iliopsoas sign =pain on extension of the right hip, which is found in retrocecal appendicitis
	- Obturator sign = pain on flexion and internal rotation of the right hip, which is seen when the inflamed appendix lies in the pelvis and causes irritation of the obturator internus muscle

### Diagnosis
- Physical exam
- Labs
	- Neutrophilic leukocytosis
	- ↑ CRP
- Abdominal CT
- Ultrasound has less accuracy

### Treatment
- Laparoscopic surgery

### Complications
- Continued progression leads to abscesses = acute suppurative appendicitis
- Even worse = Acute Gangrenous Appendicitis
- Rupture
