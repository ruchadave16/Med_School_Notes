### Etiology
- Risk lower as age increases
- 1/3 population has been infected
- Perinatal transmission, unprotected sex and IV drug use
### Pathophysiology
- HBsAg = surface antigen; appears before the onset of symptoms; continued presence indicates a carrier state or chronicity has developed.
- HBsAb = antibody to HBsAg; usually not detectable until a few weeks to months after HBsAg disappears, and may persist for life, conferring immunity to hepatitis B. By contrast, HBsAb are not produced in cases that progress to chronic disease.
- HBcAg = core protein.
- HBcAb = antibody to HBcAg; positive during the window phase of infection.
- HBeAg = appears in serum soon after HBsAg, and signifies ongoing viral replication. Persistence is indicative of progression to chronic disease, whereas development of HBeAb signify an acute infection has peaked and is waning.
