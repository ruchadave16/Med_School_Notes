### Etiology
- Primary causes
- Risk factors

### Pathophysiology
- Inflammation of gallbladder
- 90% from gallstones
	- If not, then seriously ill patients who have gallbladder stasis + ischemia

**Chronic**
- repeated episodes of acute cholecystitis 
- Calcium deposition in the gallbladder wall can lead to a condition known as porcelain gallbladder
### Clinical Features
**Acute**
- steady, severe, upper abdominal pain that radiates to the right shoulder
- Murphy's sign + -> inspiratory arrest on RUQ palpation due to pain.
- right subcostal region is markedly painful and rigid 

**Chronic**
- recurrent attacks of epigastric or right upper quadrant pain
### Diagnosis
- Ultrasound detection of gallstones + thickened gallbladder wall

### Treatment
- 25% symptomatic patients need surgery
	- If chronic, surgical removal

### Complications
- Sepsis
- Perforation -> abscess
- Fistula formation
- [[Gallbladder Carcinoma]]
