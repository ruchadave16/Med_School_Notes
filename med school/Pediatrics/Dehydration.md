>[!Note] 4 Main Questions
> - Does child need resuscitation
> - Maintenance Needs
> - Fluid deficit
> - Ongoing losses?

#### 1. Hydration and Circulation Status
- History: 
	- Initial weight before illness
	- Oral intake and outputs
	- Hyper or hypotonic fluids 
	
![[Screenshot 2026-09-08 at 11.37.56 PM.png]]

- Signs of [[Shock]] mean prioritize resuscitation and consider sepsis
	- ↓ consciousness
	- tachycardia
	- tachypnea
	- hypotension
	- ↓ pulses
	- cold extremities
	- acidosis
**Labs**
- Check electrolytes and glucose if
	- Age <6 months with poor feeding or large-volume losses
	- IV fluids or severe dehydration faced
	- Signs of electrolyte disruption
	- Medical condition predisposing to electrolyte abnormalities 
#### 2 Rehydration and Recuscitation
- Enteral first (oral or NG if mild or moderate)
- **SHOCK**
	- 10–20 mL/kg of 0.9% sodium chloride (normal saline) as fast as possible
	- Reassess after each bolus
	- In severe blood loss, might need to give RBCs
	- At ≥20 mL/kg of bolus fluid, consider other causes of shock; at ≥40 mL/kg, consider inotropes
	- Treat [[hypoglycemia]] separately from resuscitation boluses using concentrated glucose (D10–D25)

#### 3 Maintenance Fluids
- 0.9% sodium chloride (NS) most common
- Dextrose should be included in NPO child
- Potassium (commonly 20 mEq/L) only after electrolytes and renal function tested
- LR + Ceftriaxone = precipitation concern
	- Can use if line flushed before and after w saline
	- DO NOT use in pt < 28 days
- [[Tumor Lysis Syndrome]]: IV fluids guided by oncologist
- Packed RBCs + LR: administration in a separate line
- Liver failure + LR: lactate can accumulate so get expert consult

**Holliday–Segar** -- do NOT use in < 14 days
- First 10 kg 100 mL/kg/day ≈4 mL/kg/hr
- Second 10 kg 50 mL/kg/day ≈2 mL/kg/hr
- Each kg >20 kg 20 mL/kg/day ≈1 mL/kg/hr
#### 5 Deficit and Loss Replacement
Deficit replacement
- Replace half of the remaining deficit over the first 8 hours
- 2nd half over 16 hrs
- Provide maintenance fluid during the same period.

Abnormal losses
- Total hourly fluid = maintenance + ongoing-loss replacement 
- Measure the loss over a defined interval

>[!Warning]
>[[DKA]]: 
>- Signs = Polyuria and polydipsia, weight loss, new bed-wetting, vomiting and abdominal pain, Deep, rapid breathing from [[Metabolic Acidosis|metabolic acidosis]]
>  
>  Follow pediatric DKA protocol
>  - Insulin after initial fluid therapy
