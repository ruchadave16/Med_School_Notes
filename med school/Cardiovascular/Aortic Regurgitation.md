### Etiology
- Acute
	- [[Aortic dissection]] (Type A)
	- [[Infective endocarditis]]
	- Trauma
- Chronic
	- [[Congenital Bicuspid Aortic Valve]]
	- [[Rheumatic heart disease]]
	- Calcific Degeneration
	- [[Aortic Root Dilation]]
	- [[Hypertension]]
	- [[Marfan Syndrome]]
	- [[Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome|Ehlers-Danlos]]

### Pathophysiology
- Acute
	- Sudden rise in LV end diastolic pressure 
	- Rapid onset [[pulmonary edema]] and [[Cardiogenic Shock]]
- Chronic
	- Slow progressive ventricular overload 
	- LV adapts (eccentric hypertrophy)
	- Decompensation: Over many years, the sheer size and chronic volume stress lead to myocyte dysfunction, fibrosis, and a decline in contractility.

### Clinical Features
- Acute AR: 
	- Critically ill with sudden cardiovascular collapse
- Chronic AR:
	- Asymptomatic for decades
	- Early Symptoms: Awareness of a forceful heartbeat or palpitations, especially when lying down (due to increased stroke volume)
	- Late Symptoms (Signs of [[Heart Failure]])
		- Dyspnea on exertion
		- Angina
### Diagnosis
- Physical Exam
	- Murmur: Soft, high-pitched, blowing, decrescendo early diastolic murmur
	- Wide pulse pressure: Hallmark hemodynamic finding (high systolic and low diastolic)
		- Corrigan's Pulse -- A rapid, forceful upstroke and collapse of the carotid pulse
		- de Musset's Sign -- Head bobbing with each heartbeat
		- Quincke's Pulse -- Capillary pulsations visible in the nail bed
		- Traube's Sign -- Pistol shot" sounds heard over the femoral artery
- TTE
	- Measures severity of the leak (regurgitant volume and fraction), LV size (end-diastolic and end-systolic dimensions), and LV systolic function (ejection fraction - EF) for timing surgical intervention
- Cardiac MRI:
	- when echo is suboptimal
- ECG
- Chest Xray
### Treatment
- Pharmacologic
	- Afterload Reduction:
		- [[Vasodilators]]
- Surgical
	- Acute = surgical emergency
	- Asymptomatic severe AR with LVEF ≤55%: Surgery (Class I)
		- LVESD >50 mm or indexed LVESD >25 mm/m²: Surgery (Class IIa)
		- Progressive decline in LVEF to 55–60% on ≥3 serial studies: Surgery (Class IIb)
		- TAVR is explicitly contraindicated (Class III: Harm) for isolated severe AR with surgical indications
- Lifestyle
