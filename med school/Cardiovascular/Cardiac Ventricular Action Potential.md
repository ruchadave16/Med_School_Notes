- Lasts almost as long as the contraction of the heart
- Extracellular Ca2+ triggers the release of Ca2+ from [[Sarcoplasmic Reticulum|sarcoplasmic reticulum]] of heart

#### Phases
**Phase 4:** [[Resting Potential|Resting potential]] is when $K^+$ conductance is high and sodium gates have *not* opened
	→ Close to -90mV
	
**Phase 0:** Depolarization because of *fast sodium* channels opening
- These are voltage and timing dependent
	- Inactivate automatically
- Threshold = -60 mV
- Overshoots up to about 20-30 mV
- Influx of Na+ 
	- Drives potential towards +70 but it doens't get there because small amt of K+ leaves as well
	
![[Screenshot 2026-04-29 at 2.18.51 PM.png]]

**Phase 1:** 
- Repolarization because sodium channels go inactive and potassium goes out
- Outward current from volt-gated K+ channels 

**Phase 2:** 
- Plateau because calcium voltage gated open and calcium influx balances out the potassium efflux
	- This causes the action potential to last **much** longer than in neurons
- **L-type** Ca2+ channels that are found in [[T Tubules|T-tubules]] especially
	- These are activated by **PKA** from $\beta1$

**Phase 3:** 
- Repolarization because calcium channels close (time dependent) and potassium efflux continued to bring the polarization back 
- K+ channels are slow
