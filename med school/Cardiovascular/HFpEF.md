### Etiology
- Long-standing [[hypertension]], obesity, aging, diabetes, [[Restrictive Cardiomyopathy|restrictive cardiomyopathy]]
- Chronotropic incompetence -> ~50% patients

### Pathophysiology
- EF > 50%
- Stiff, non-compliant ventricle -> diastolic dysfunction
- [[PV Diagrams|PV diagrams]] loop shifts **up and to left**

### Clinical Features
- Exertional dyspnea (35% have no other signs)
- Fatigue
- Edema
- S4 may be present

### Diagnosis
- H2FPEF Scoring System: The H2FPEF score (range 0–9) is the most practical and validated tool, using 6 readily available variables: 
	- Heavy (BMI >30): 2 points
	- Hypertensive (≥2 antihypertensive medications): 1 point
	- AF (atrial fibrillation): 3 points
	- Pulmonary hypertension (echo PASP >35 mmHg): 1 point
	- Elder (age >60): 1 point
	- Filling pressure (E/e′ >9): 1 point
	- *A score ≥6 is highly suggestive of HFpEF (>90% probability); a score 0–1 makes HFpEF unlikely. Intermediate scores (2–5) warrant further evaluation, ideally with exercise hemodynamic testing.*
- TTE:
	- Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF) ≥ 50%.
	- Elevated E/e' ratio on Doppler imaging
- Labs
	- Elevated Natriuretic Peptides  -- these are pretty significant so if normal, HFpEF is unlikely
- Right heart catheterization
	- At Rest: PCWP may be normal (≤ 15 mmHg) or elevated (> 15-18 mmHg)
	- High filling pressures despite normal forward flow
	- Exaggerated rise in PCWP with exercise (often >25 mmHg)
### Treatment
- Manage comorbidities
- [[Diuretics]] -- symptomatic
- [[SGLT2 inhibitors]]
- [[Finerenone]] (FINEARTS-HF trial (2024)) showed that composite of total worsening HF events and CV death by 16% (rate ratio 0.84) in patients with HF and EF ≥40%
- If obesity, [[GLP-1 agonists]]
- Exercise training **

>[!Note] No real benefit of [[beta blockers]]
