*Depolarization and repolarization of myocardium causes V differences that can be detected on skin surface*

- Dipole is measured between 2 leads
#### 12-Lead
- 6 limb leads, 6 precordial leads
- 3 bipolar (I, II, III) and 3 unipolar (aVR, aVL, and aVF)
	- Assessed using 4 electrodes on left arm, R arm, L leg, R leg
	- Modified version exists with placement on torso instead of arms
	
>[!Tip]
>Voltage difference between any placements will get us the values we need

#### Reading an ECG
Each lead measures the dipole projected onto that leads axis
- Y-axis = blocks of 0.1 mV and large blocks of 0.5 mV
	- Calibration = 10 mm/mV
- X-axis = time blocks of 0.04s and large of 0.2 s
	- Speed = 25 mm/s
	
![[Screenshot 2026-05-01 at 10.55.43 PM.png]]

**Waves**
- P wave: atrial depolarization
	- PR = time between atrial and ventricular depolarization
	- 
	- *normal: 0.12–0.20 s, 3-5 little boxes*
- QRS: ventricular depolarization
	- QT: time for ventricles to depolarize and completely repolarize
	- *resting normal: ~0.35–0.45 s, ~2 big boxes; may be slightly longer in women*
	- QRS: time for depolarization wave to go through ventricles
	- *normal: 0.08–0.10 s, 2-2.5 little boxes*
- T: ventricular repolarization
- RR Interval: time between heart beats
	- *resting normal: ~0.6–1.0 s, 3-5 big boxes*

### Calculation
#### [[Heart Rate]]
- Count # QRS segments * 6 = beats per minute for 10 second strip
- If regular rhythm
	- 60 / RR interval
	- 300 / # large boxes in RR interval
#### Mean QRS
1) net amplitude of QRS of Lead I and aVF
	- peak in mV above the baseline (peak of R) - nadir in mV below the baseline (base of S)
	- If 0, QRS = isoelectric
2) plot the net amplitude as vectors along the Lead I and aVF axes
3) using vector addition, plot the resulting vector that represents the mean QRS axis
