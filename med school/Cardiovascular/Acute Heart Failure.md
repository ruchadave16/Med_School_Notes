**Medical Emergency**
### Etiology
- 20% of cases are de novo, rest are from [[chronic heart failure]] that decompensated
- MI
- [[AFib]]
- Infection
- Hypertensive crisis
- Acute regurg due to Type A [[Aortic Dissection|aortic dissection]]

- High salt intake
- Poor med compliance
- [[NSAIDs]] (low renal perfusion)
### Pathophysiology
- Frank-Starling curve shifts to **right and down**:
	- For any preload, SV is much much lower (high LVEDP)
- SV ↓ means CO ↓ so huge [[Sympathetic Nervous System|sympathetic]] surge
	- ↑↑ SVR and afterload, which worsens LV causing a worsening loop
- [[Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System|RAAS system]] is also activated, causing further fluid retention
- [[BNP]] released in response to stretch and wall stress (high LVEDP)

### Clinical Features
- Pulmonary congestion: Severe dyspnea, orthopnea, [[paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea]] (PND), pink frothy sputum
- Auscultation: rales, **[[S3 gallop]]**
- Hypoperfusion

**Classifications**
*Axes: Perfusion ("Warm" vs. "Cold") and Congestion ("Wet" vs. "Dry").*
- Profile A: Warm and Dry -- normal
- Profile B: Warm and Wet -- most common, good perfusion, but volume overloaded.
- Profile C: Cold and Wet -- Poor perfusion and volume overloaded.
- Profile L: Cold and Dry -- Poor perfusion, euvolemic. (‘L’ stands for Low CO)

*INTERMACS Profiles: Classifies patients with advanced HF by severity, primarily to*
*determine candidacy for mechanical circulatory support* 
- Profile 1: Critical Cardiogenic Shock ("Crash and Burn"): The patient has life-threatening low blood pressure and organ failure despite heavy medication. They need life-saving intervention within hours.
- Profile 2: Progressive Decline ("Sliding on Inotropes"): The patient is dependent on IV medications (inotropes) but is still getting worse, with failing kidneys or nutrition.
- Profile 3: Stable but Inotrope Dependent ("Dependent Stability"): The patient is stable but cannot be taken off IV medications without their blood pressure dropping or symptoms returning.
- Profile 4: Resting Symptoms ("Recurrent Advanced HF"): The patient is at home on oral pills but has symptoms like shortness of breath or swelling even while resting or doing simple things like dressing.
- Profile 5: Exertion Intolerant ("Housebound"): The patient is comfortable sitting still but cannot do any physical activity without becoming exhausted or short of breath.
- Profile 6: Exertion Limited ("Walking Wounded"): The patient is comfortable at rest and can do minor activity, but they fatigue very quickly and often have "frequent flyer" hospital visits.
- Profile 7: Advanced NYHA Class III (see below in Chronic HF for details): The patient is stable and can walk more than a block but still has significant heart disease that limits their daily life. 

*SCAI SHOCK STAGING SYSTEM* 
- Stage A: At risk but hemodynamically stable
- Stage B: Hypotension OR hypoperfusion, but not both; no drugs/devices needed yet
- Stage C: Hypotension AND hypoperfusion
- Stage D: Failing to respond to initial interventions; escalating support
- Stage E: Circulatory collapse, refractory shock
### Diagnosis
**Differential: Rule out [[ACS]]**
- [[BNP]] (if this is normal, AHF HIGHLY unlikely; high means most likely)
- [[Troponins]]
- BMP
	- Elevated creatine
	- Hyponatremia
	- Monitor potassium
	- Calcium and magnesium monitoring
- LFTs
- Iron Studies: Iron deficiency is present in ~50% of HF patients 

>[!Note] **[[Sacubitril]]** stops breakdown of BNP so in patients on this med, BNP will be artificially high. Measure NT-proBNP instead

>[!Note] Obesity
>Can suppress production of NP 
> Standard NT-proBNP rule-out threshold of <125 ng/L had only 67% sensitivity in patients with BMI >35 kg/m².

- Chest X-ray (ABCDE)
	- Alveolar edema: Fluffy, indistinct opacities, often in a perihilar "bat wing" or "butterfly" distribution
	- Kerley B Lines: Short, horizontal lines seen in the lung periphery 
	- Cardiomegaly
	- Cephalization of Pulmonary Vessels:
	- Pleural effusions
- ECG
- Lung Ultrasound
	-  more sensitive to identify cardiogenic pulmonary edema
- TTE:
	- Asses LV EF
	- Wall motion abnormalities
	- Check structural causes
- Right heart catheterization: not routine first step
	- Pulmonary Capillary Wedge Pressure (PCWP) elevated
	- CO low
	- SVR high
### Treatment
**Goal: Reduce preload and or increase contractility**

- Profile B:  [[Diuretics]] (torsemide or furosemide), [[vasodilators]]  ([[nitroglycerin]])
- Profile C: [[Diuretics]] (torsemide or furosemide), [[vasopressors]] 
- Profile L: Fluids and/or [[vasopressors]]

>[!Note]
> For patients already on oral loop diuretics at home: start with an IV dose of 1–2.5× the home oral dose
> - Can add [[metolazone]] to give loop diuretics a kick
> - Can add [[Nitrates]]

- IV iron regardless of anemia status
- [[SGLT2 inhibitors]] once hemodynamically stable
- [[NIPPV]] for pulmonary edema

>[!Warning] 
>NO NIPPV if vomiting, cardiogenic shock, or obtunded

- [[Beta Agonist|beta 1 agonist]] or [[PDE Inhibitors|PDE 3 inhibitors]] -- only for acute therapy
### Complications
- Cardiorenal syndrome
- Hyponatremia -- poor prognosis
