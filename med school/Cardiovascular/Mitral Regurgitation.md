### Etiology
- Primary
	- [[Mitral Valve Prolapse ]]
	- [[Rheumatic heart disease]]
	- [[Infective endocarditis]]
	- [[Ruptured Chordae Tendineae]]
	- [[Ruptured Papillary Muscle]] -- post [[Myocardial Infarction|MI]] within 2-7 days
- Secondary
	- Ischemic MR: Post [[Myocardial Infarction|MI]] remodeling 
	- [[Dilated Cardiomyopathy]]
	- [[Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy]]

### Pathophysiology
- Flow of blood backwards from LV to LA during systole 
- Acute
	- Large V blood ejected into non-compliant LA so ↑ LA pressure and pulmonary venous pressure.
- Chronic
	- LV blood into aorta and LA (low pressure)
	- LV and LA dilate
		- LV = eccentric hypertrophy
		- LA = massively dilated and compliant.
	- **EF can look normal** 
- Decompensation
	- Progressive LV dilation, contractile failure, rising LA pressures

### Clinical Features
- Acute
	- Critically ill with sudden, severe dyspnea, hypotension, and shock
- Chronic
	- Long asymptomatic phase
	- Fatigue + decreased exercise tolerance

### Diagnosis
- Physical exam
	- Murmur: A soft, high-pitched, blowing holosystolic (or pansystolic) murmur.
		- Apex -> axilla
	- ↓ S1, wide splitting S2, S3 gallop
	- PMI displaced and hyperdynamic
- TTE / TEE
- ECG
- CXR
### Treatment
- Pharmacologic
	- [[Vasodilators]] so ↑ forward flow
	- [[Diuretics]]: To control symptoms of pulmonary congestion.
- Surgical
	- ALL symptomatic patients with severe chronic MR.
	- Asymptomatic patients with severe MR and evidence of LV dysfunction (**EF ≤ 60%**) or significant LV dilation (**LVESD ≥ 40 mm**)

	- Mitral Valve Repair **Preferred**
	- Mitral Valve Replacement
	- TEER 
- Lifestyle
