### Etiology
- ~4-8% males over 65 years-old
- 4:1 M:F ratio
- >60 yrs = risk
- up to 20% have genetic factor
	- Matrix metalloproteinases ([[MMPs]]), especially MMP-2 and MMP-9
	- Fibrillin-1 ([[FBN1]]), also implicated in [[Marfan Syndrome]]
	- [[Collagen]] genes (e.g., COL3A1 in vascular Ehlers-Danlos síndrome)

**Risk factors**
- **Smoking**
- [[Hypertension]]
- [[Atherosclerosis]]
- [[Hyperlipidemia]]
- Family history of AAA

### Pathophysiology
- Localized dilation of abdominal aorta > 3 cm
	- Common between renal and iliac arteries
- Aneurysm of all 3 layers
	- Fusiform in shape
- Dissection = rare
- Varying levels of ILT
- Variants
	- inflammatory AAA (younger, back pain, ↑inflammatory cells, periaortic scarring
	- mycotic AAA (misnomer because usually bacteria, medial destruction, rapid growth)
	- IgG4-Related AAA (↑IgG4, tissue fibrosis, plasma cells, aortitis, steroids help)

### Clinical Features
- Asymptomatic
- If rupture, acute abdominal / back pain 

### Diagnosis
- **Ultrasound** = gold standard
	- **Recommended for men 65-75 who have smoked**
	- Serial imaging between 6-24 months
- CT angiography

### Treatment
- Surgical intervention for high risk
	- Maximum diameter ≥5.5 cm in men or ≥5.0 cm in women
	- Rapid growth (>0.5 cm in 6 months)
	- Symptomatic AAA
	- Rupture
- Smoking cessation, blood pressure control, [[Statins|statin]] therapy
- Surgery
	- Endovascular Aortic Repair
	- Open Surgical Repair 
### Complications
- If rupture, 90% mortality rate
