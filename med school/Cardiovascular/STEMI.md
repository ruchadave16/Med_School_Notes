*Complete occlusion of artery -> transmural necrosis*
- Severe pain, including at rest
- Can radiate widely -- [[Myocardial Infarction|MI]]
#### **ECG**
- Minutes: Hyperacute T waves = earliest sign
- Minutes to hours: **ST segment elevation** begins (≥1mm in **2 contiguous leads**)
- Hours: ST elevation progresses; Q waves may begin to develop
- 12–24 hours: ST elevation persists; Q waves deepen; T wave inversion begins
- Days to weeks: ST elevation resolves; T wave inversion deepens; Q waves persist
- Weeks to months: ST segment normalizes; T waves may normalize or remain inverted; Q waves persist as permanent markers of infarction

>[!Note] If Persistent ST elevation weeks after MI → suspect ventricular aneurysm

- New [[LBBB]]
	- ≥1 mm STE concordant with QRS (Sensitivity 73%, Specificity 92%) 
	- STD ≥1 mm in V1–V3
	- STE ≥5 mm discordant with QRS 
	- Modified Sgarbossa (Smith criteria): Replaces the third criterion with STE/S wave ratio ≥0.25 
	
**Markers**
- [[Troponin]] (hs-cTn)
	- Peak at 12-24 hrs
- CK-MB
	- Rises 3–4 hours after MI, peaks at 12–24 hours, normalizes by 48–72 hours
- Myoglobin, BNP, LDH

#### STEMI Reperfusion
- PRIMARY [[PCI]]: Goal ≤90 min (door-to-balloon)
	- mechanically opens the occlusion
- FIBRINOLYSIS if not available in 120 min
	- [[tPA|Alteplase]] (tPA), tenecteplase (TNK), reteplase (within 30 min)
	- Goal: door-to-needle ≤30 min
	- Contraindications: prior ICH, active bleeding, recent surgery, severe HTN (>185/110)
	- Successful lysis: ≥50% ST resolution + reperfusion arrhythmias
- Adjunct Therapy:
	- [[Aspirin]] + [[P2Y12 Inhibitors]]
	- [[Anticoagulants]]
