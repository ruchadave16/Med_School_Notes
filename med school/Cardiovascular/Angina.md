*Chest pain or discomfort when oxygen demand > oxygen supply to myocardium*
- Commonly due to [[coronary artery disease]]
### [[Stable Angina]]
- Partial obstruction (generally > 70% occlusion)
- Pain upon exertion 
	- Relieved upon rest and delivery of sublingual [[nitroglycerin]] in 5-10 minutes
- ECG normal, might show ST depression
- Troponin negative
- *First Line*: [[Beta Blockers]] and [[Nitrates]]
- Add CCBs or ranolazine if needed
### Unstable Angina
- Plaque disruption but not completely occluded
- New onset, increase in severity, pain at rest
- Normal or can see ST depression on ECG
- Negative troponin
- Admit and give [[antiplatelets]], [[anticoagulants]], [[nitrates]], [[beta blockers]]
### Vasospastic Angina (Prinzmetal's)
- Pain at rest, often nocturnal
- Transient ST elevation during the period but arteries normal when cath
- Seen in young woman and smokers
- Treat: [[CCBs]] and [[nitrates]]
	- Avoid **beta blockers**
- Diagnosis: Provocative testing with acetylcholine or ergonovine during angiography
### Silent Ischemia
- Objective ischemia with no symptoms
- Seen in [[diabetes]] and elderly
- Might see fatigue, dyspnea
- Detect on stress test
### Microvascular Angina (Cardiac Syndrome X)
- Angina and ST change on stress test but normal coronaries
- Seen common in women
- Impaired coronary microvascular function

#### **Treatments**
1) Relieve Anginal Symptoms
2) Prevent Ischemic Events
3) Reduce Mortality 

Reduce Demand
- [[Beta Blockers|beta blocker]] : ↓ HR, ↓ contractility, ↓ BP → ↓ demand (most important effect); also ↑ diastolic filling time → ↑ supply
- [[Nitrates]]: ↓ preload (venodilation) → ↓ wall stress → ↓ demand; also coronary vasodilation → ↑ supply
- [[Non-dihydropyridines|Non-DHPs]]: ↓ afterload, ↓ HR (non-dihydropyridines), coronary vasodilation → ↓ demand + ↑ supply
- [[Ranolazine]]: Inhibits late sodium current → reduces intracellular calcium overload → ↓ wall tension during diastole

Improve Supply
- [[Dihydropyridines|DHPs]]: ↑ coronary vasodilation
- [[Nitrates]]: ↑ coronary blood flow
- [[Antiplatelets]] and [[statins]]: ↓ progression of atherosclerosis
