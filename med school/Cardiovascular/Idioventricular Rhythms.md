### Etiology
- A rhythm originating from the ventricular conduction system that occurs when higher-level pacemakers (SA and AV nodes) have failed (escape rhythm) or when a ventricular focus becomes transiently irritable (AIVR).
- [[Escape Rhythm]]
	- The ventricles' intrinsic pacemaker takes over due to complete failure of all higher pacemakers. This is a "rhythm of last resort" seen in severe [[conduction system disease]].
- [[AIVR]]
	- focus becomes irritable and fires faster. Classically seen during reperfusion therapy for an [[Myocardial Infarction|MI]] (e.g., after angioplasty), as the restored blood flow to ischemic tissue makes it electrically unstable.

### Diagnosis
- [[ECG]]
	- A regular rhythm with wide QRS complexes (>0.12s) and no preceding P waves. 
	- Ventricular [[Escape Rhythm]]: 20-40 bpm (the ventricle's intrinsic rate).
	- Accelerated Idioventricular Rhythm ([[AIVR]]): 40-100 bpm (faster than escape, slower than V-Tach).
### Treatment
- Escape Rhythm
	- Prepare for emergency pacing and treat the underlying severe bradycardia
	- **Do not suppress this rhythm.**
- AIVR
	- Benign, observe but don't treat unless needed
