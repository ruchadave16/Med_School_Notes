#### Features
- Striated, single cells
- Elongated, branched cells with [[intercalated discs]]
- *Central nuclei*
- Less organized than skeletal muscles
- Numerous, large [[mitochondria]] and [[glycogen]] stores

**Involuntary** and rhythmic contractions
- [[Intercalated discs]] (in arrows) linking cells

>[!Note] [[Pacemaker Cells]]
>These cells are the ones that are start the contraction and are impacted by the sympathetic / parasympathetic systems. The method of contraction, channels, and cardiac cycle are different than normal ventricular myocytes

#### Contraction Methods
Similar to [[Skeletal Muscle Contractions]]
- Myosin and actin, [[tropomyosin]] inhibition, Ca2+ regulation
- Same [[Sarcomere|sarcomeres]]
- Same cross-bridge method

Differences:
- Intrinsic and spontaneous contraction
	- Does not depend on external signal but can be changed based on signal
- Contraction lasts as long as the action potential
- Coupled electrically so every cell contracts with each heartbeat
	- **Contractile force ↑ by increasing Ca2+ level and stretch in diastole**
#### Cardiac [[Myofibril|Myofibrils]]
![[Screenshot 2026-04-28 at 10.42.08 PM.png]]

- Same functions but less developed and irregular
	- Does NOT separate myofilaments into myofibrils here
- [[T Tubules]] at Z-Line here 
	- Has Na+ and Ca2+ channels
	- Allows for **simultaneous** activation of heart 
- **Diad** NOT triad
- Extracellular Ca2+ triggers the release of Ca2+ from [[sarcoplasmic reticulum]]
	- The more the Ca2+, the stronger the [[Excitation-Contraction Coupling]]
