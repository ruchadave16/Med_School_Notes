- Hemagglutinin (HA) -- binds to sialic acid, viral entry and neuraminidase (NA) -- virion release
	- H1 - H3
	- N1 - N2
- **Negative sense Segmented RNA (8 segments)**
- Enveloped
#### Mechanism
- Spread by respiratory droplets

- M2 protein = proton channel to change pH for viral encoding -- Influenza A
- NA cleaves sialic acid to release virion

>[!Note] Resistance from mutation of NA gene

- HA targeted by antibodies, attaches to sialic acid receptors

- Genetic shift -- 2 different segmented viruses infect 1 cell -> RNA segment reassortment -> dramatically different virus so ↑ pandemic
- Genetic drive -- random mutation in HA or NA -> minor change causing seasonal outbreak
###### Replication
- **EXCEPTION to RNA viruses: these actually replicate in nucleus**
- HA binds to sialic acid on cell membrane -- phagocytosis into cell
- Fusion and releases vRNPs, which travel to nucleus
- Transcription of mRNA
	- RNA dependent RNA polymerase does cap-snatching, mRNA comes out and is converted to proteins
- Replication
	- vRNA (-) -> cRNA (+)
	- cRNA (+) -> new vRNA (-)
#### Symptoms
- Myalgias, high fever
- Can cause pneumonia
- GI symptom in children
- ↑ susceptibility to [[Staph Aureus|staph aureus]], [[Streptococcus Pneumonia|Strep Pneumo]], [[Hemophilus Influenza|H. influenza]]

Rare
- [[Guillain Barre Syndrome|Guillain-Barre syndrome]]
- [[Reyes Syndrome]]

Mortality 90% in adults >75 years
#### Diagnosis
#### Treatment
Start immediately in hospitalized patients or high risk

- [[Oseltamivir]] / [[anamivir]], [[Oseltamivir|zanamivir]] inhibits NA -- no virion release
	- Shortens illness by 18–25 hours
	- Reduces lower respiratory tract complications by ~44%
- [[Baloxavir]], [[marboxil]] = cap-dependent endonuclease inhibitor 
	- no viral mRNA synthesis

NOT IN USE because of resistance
- [[Amantadine]] / rimantadine inhibit M2 -- no uncoating

>[!Warning] [[Aspirin]] is CONTRAINDICATED in viral illness for kids
>[[Reyes Syndrome]]
>- Could cause liver failure
>- [[Viral encephalitis]]
#### Prevention
- Reformulated vaccine ("flu shot") yearly
	- Killed vaccine most commonly used
	- Live attenuated given intranasally
		- Not for immunocompromised, pregnant, or age <2 or >49
	- High dose and adjunct vaccine
		- For age ≥65; improves immunogenicity
- Hand hygiene, respiratory etiquette
