### Etiology
- **Most common MND** -- 2/3 in 100,000
- Mostly idiopathic
- Genetic: GGGGCC (intron 1)
	- Normal = 2-25
	- Abnormal = 30-several thousand
- Environmental
	- Lead, cigarettes
- Newer research suggests association with pathogenic variant in C9ORF72 gene in up 70 % of cases
- Onset: 45-55 yrs (familial), 65+ sporadic
### Pathophysiology
- Progressive pyramidal motor neuron loss (**UMN and LMN**) and loss in ventral horn
- Most die in 3-5 years of respiratory failure
### Clinical Features
- Symptoms between 55-75 years
- **NO sensory**, bowel/bladder deficits
- Slow progression
- [[UMN Lesion]]– dysarthria, dysphagia, spastic gait, clonus
	- Diaphragmatic weakness
- [[LMN Lesion]]  – asymmetrical weakness, fasciculations, atrophy; dysarthria, dysphagia
- [[Frontotemporal dementia]]
- Anxiety / depression
### Diagnosis
- Detailed neuro exam with UMN and LMN signs
- EMG and NCV to rule out myopathy, peripheral neuropathy, synaptopathy
- Blood and urine test for heavy metals, thyroid, parathyroid
- X-ray, myelogram, MRI
- Muscle / nerve biopsy
- Genetic testing

**Differential:** lead toxicity, dysproteinemia, thyroid dysfunction, B12 deficiency, [[vasculitis]], paraneoplastic syndromes, hexosaminidase A deficiency, multifocal neuropathy with conduction block, spine compression

### Treatment
- Pharmacologic
	- Riluzole reduces progression
- Surgical
- Lifestyle

### Complications
- Death from respiratory failure in 3-5 years
- 5-20% have [[Frontotemporal Dementia|frontotemporal dementia]]
