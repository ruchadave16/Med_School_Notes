*Measure potential between 2 electrodes*

- Depolarization goes from endocardium to epicardium (moves towards + node) so upwards
- Repolarization goes from epicardium to endocardium but is a negative change so both cancel and upwards bump
### How to Read
![[Screenshot 2026-05-20 at 9.53.36 PM.png]]
![[Screenshot 2026-05-20 at 9.53.53 PM.png]]
1) Quality Assessment
	- Patient: Is this the correct patient (name/DOB/MRN)?
	- Date/Time: Is this the correct ECG?
	- Calibration: Normal is Speed: 25 mm/second, Voltage: 10 mm/millivolt (mV)
	- Quality: Check for artifacts, signs of lead reversal (negative P-wave in Lead I, extreme axis, abnormal R-wave progression)
2) Rate
	- R - R intervals (300 / # large boxes)
	- ECG rhythm strip lasts 10- seconds. Count the number of QRS complexes on the strip and multiply by 6. This yields the average beats per minute and works for irregular rhythms
	- Normal: 60–100 bpm; Tachycardia: >100; Bradycardia: 60
3) Rhythm
	- P waves
		- Are P waves present? 
		- Rate, PP interval, morphology and axis (normally upright in I, II, aVF)
		- Location of P-waves relative to QRS complexes; Find PR interval (normal <200 ms)
		- Ratio of P-waves to QRS: Is there a QRS for every P wave? P-wave for every QRS?
	- QRS Complex
		- Duration (normal < 120 s)
		- Is the rhythm regular or irregular?
	- Regular + P waves = sinus; Irregularly irregular + no P waves = [[AFib|atrial fibrillation]]
4) Axis
	- Look at leads I and aVF
		• Both positive = normal axis (0° to +90°)
		- Lead I +, Lead II - = LAD 
		- Lead I -, aVF + = RAD
		- Lead I - and aVF - = extreme
	- R wave progression
		- R-wave should inc. from V1-V6; becomes > S-wave ~V3-V4
		- Poor R-wave progression – R-wave becomes dominant more posterolaterally 

5) Intervals
	- PR interval: 120–200 ms (>200 ms = 1st degree AV block; 120 ms = pre- excitation/WPW)
		- Short < 120 ms = Accelerated depolarization of ventricles relative to atria
		- Long > 200 ms = Delayed depolarization of ventricles relative to atria
	- QRS duration: 120 ms normal; 120–150 ms = incomplete BBB; >150 ms = complete BBB
		- Wide = slow conduction through ventricles
	- QT interval: ~ < 1/2 of preceeding R-R interval
		- Corrected QTc 440 ms (men), 460 ms (women); 
		- Prolonged QTc → risk of [[Torsades de Pointes]]
		
		![[Screenshot 2026-05-25 at 2.07.43 PM.png]]
5) Morphology
	- Hypertrophy
		- [[Left Ventricular Hypertrophy]] (LVH) = excessively tall QRS complex
			- Sokolow-Lyon Criteria: S wave depth in V1 + R wave height in V5 or V6 > 35 mm. 
		- [[Right Ventricular Hypertrophy]] = dominant R wave in V1 (taller than S wave)
	- Ischemia, Injury, and Infarction 
		- Look at the ECG by territories (e.g., Inferior: II, III, aVF; Anterior: V1-V4; Lateral: I, aVL, V5, V6) and recall coronary artery distribution
		- Q waves: Pathologic if >40 ms wide or >25% of R wave amplitude → prior MI
		- ST segment: Elevation or depression (see ischemia section)
			- [[ST Elevation]]
			- ST Depression: Myocardial ischemia, acute true posterior MI (V1-V3), Digitalis effect, Hypokalemia
		- T waves: Inversion, hyperacute (tall/peaked), flattening
			- Inversion: Ischemia or infarct, Myopericarditis, cardiomyopathy, Repolarization abnormality

### Common Conditions
- [[STEMI]]
	- ST-segment elevation (≥1 mm) in two or more contiguous leads
- [[Vfib]]
	- A chaotic, wavy line with no discernible P waves, QRS complexes, or T waves.
- [[VTach]]
	- A regular, fast (>100 bpm) rhythm with wide QRS complexes (>0.12s).
- [[Torsades de Pointes]]
	- A very fast, polymorphic V-Tach where the QRS amplitude appears to "twist" around the isoelectric baseline.
- [[Asystole]]
	- "Flatline" -- no cardiac output
	- NON-shockable
	- Code blue and start [[CPR]]
- [[Complete Heart Block|3rd degree heart block]]
	- P and QRS present but have no relationship 
- [[NSTEMI]] / [[Unstable Angina]]
	- ST-segment depression or dynamic T-wave inversions in contiguous leads.
- [[Idioventricular Rhythms]]: regular rhythm with wide QRS complexes (>0.12s) and no preceding P waves
	- [[Escape Rhythm]] -- 20-40 bpm 
	- [[AIVR]] -- 40 - 100 bpm
- [[Pulmonary Embolism|PE]]
	- The most common finding is [[sinus tachycardia]]. The "classic" but rare S1Q3T3 pattern is a large S wave in I, a deep Q in III, and an inverted T in III.
- [[AFib]]
	- An "irregularly irregular" rhythm with no discernible P waves.
- [[Atrial Flutter]]
	- A classic "sawtooth" pattern of flutter waves, often with a regular ventricular response (e.g., 2:1 block)
- [[SVT]]
	-  regular, narrow-complex (<0.12s) tachycardia, typically with a rate >150 bpm where P waves are often hidden.
- [[Mobitz I]] -- 2nd degree AV block
	- Progressive lengthening of the PR interval until a QRS complex is dropped
- [[Mobitz II]] -- 2nd degree AV block
	- A consistent PR interval in the conducted beats followed by a suddenly dropped QRS complex without warning
- [[Hyperkalemia]]
	- The classic progression starts with tall, peaked T waves, which can evolve to a widened QRS and a "sine wave" pattern.
- [[WPW Syndrome]]
	- The classic triad is a short PR interval (<0.12s), a delta wave (slurred upstroke of the QRS), and a widened QRS complex.
- [[Brugada Syndrome]]
	- The diagnostic Type 1 Brugada pattern is a coved ST-segment elevation (>2mm) followed by an inverted T-wave in leads V1 and/or V2
- [[ARVC]]
	- The pathognomonic finding is an Epsilon wave, a small positive deflection (a "blip") at the very end of the QRS complex, best seen in V1. Also, T-wave inversions in the right precordial leads (V1-V3)
- [[LBBB]]
	- A wide QRS (>0.12s) with a deep S wave in V1 ("W") and a broad, notched R wave in V6 ("M").
- [[RBBB]]
	- A wide QRS (>0.12s) with an RSR' ("rabbit ears") pattern in V1 and a wide, slurred S wave in V6
- [[Sinus Tachycardia]]
	- A normal P wave for every QRS, with a regular rhythm and rate >100 bpm.
- [[MAT]]
	- The strict diagnostic criteria are: 1) Rate > 100 bpm, 2) At least 3 distinct P wave morphologies, and 3) Variable P-P, P-R, and R-R intervals. It is an irregularly irregular rhythm with visible P waves (distinguishing it from Afib). 
	- *Note: A Wandering Pacemaker has similar criteria but has a rate <100 bpm.
- [[Sinus Bradycardia]]
	- A normal P wave for every QRS, with a regular rhythm and rate <60 bpm.
- [[Sinus Arrest]]
	- A sudden pause in the rhythm that is not a multiple of the normal P-P interval, with no P wave visible during the pause.
- [[Junctional Rhythm]]
	- A regular, narrow-complex rhythm at a slow rate (40-60 bpm). P waves are either absent, inverted and immediately preceding the QRS, or buried within the QRS.
- [[Premature Atrial Contraction]]
	- A premature P wave (often with a different morphology than the sinus P wave) that is followed by a QRS complex. It "resets" the sinus rhythm.
- [[Premature Ventricular Contraction]]
	- A premature, wide (>0.12s), and bizarre-looking QRS complex that is not preceded by a P wave. Often followed by a "full compensatory pause.
