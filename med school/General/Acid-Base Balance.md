[[Acidemia]] – blood pH ↓, depresses [[CNS]] (lowest = 6.8 for survival)
[[Alkalemia]] – blood pH ↑, excites [[CNS]] (highest = 7.7 for survival)

[[Acidosis]] – net gain of $H^+$ in fluids
[[Alkalosis]] – net loss of $H^+$ in fluids

Acids produced through metabolism **NORMAL pH = 7.4**
1) volatile $H_2CO_3$ from $CO_2$ = 15,000 mmol/day
2) nonvolatile acids from sulfur containing + phosphorylated AA
#### Regulation
1) [[Buffers]] : systems regulate immediately to minimize pH change
2) [[Lungs]] ([[Respiratory Regulation of Acid-Base|respiratory regulation]]): min-to-min regulation by changing $CO_2$ removal rate
3) [[Kidneys]] ([[Renal Regulation of Acid-Base|renal regulation]]): hours-to-days regulation that changes
	- Rate of renal tubular $H^+$ transport from tubular cell to renal tubule
	- Rate of $HCO_3^-$ reabsorption from tubule into tubular cell and $HCO_3^-$ formation by renal cell

### MANAGEMENT
Think of: GOLDMARK
- Glycols, Oxoproline, L-lactate, D-lactate, Methanol, Aspirin, Renal failure, Ketoacidosis

1) Acidosis or alkalosis
2) Respiratory or metabolic
3) Is compensation normal
4) Anion gap
	- AG = Na+ - (Cl- + HCO3-)
	- Adjusted normal based on [[Albumin]]: 12 + 2.5 mEq/L for each g/dL above 4.5 g/dL, 12 – 2.5 mEq/L for each g/dL below 4.5 g/dL
5) Delta-Delta (if Anion Gap TRUE)
	- Measured anion gap – Normal anion gap / (Normal HCO3- – Measured HCO3-)
		- If ratio <1: Anion gap acidosis AND Normal gap acidosis
		- If ratio >1: Anion gap acidosis AND Concurrent metabolic alkalosis
6) Osmolal Gap
	- Measured plasma osmolality (mOsm)
	- cOsm = 2 $*$ Na + BUN / 2.8 + Glu / 18
	- If mOsm - cOsm > 10, may be an unaccounted for osmole present

ALTERNATE: Urine anion gap if NO anion gap: 
- Urine AG = Urine (Na+ + K+) – Cl-
- If ammonium excretion impaired, Na+ + K+ >> Cl-
	- UAG + which is [[renal tubular acidosis]]
- Else diarrhea (UAG -)
