### Etiology
- Chronic ear infections
- Ear surgery
- Can be congenital

### Pathophysiology
- Non-cancerous overgrowth of keratinized squamous epithelium in middle ear
- Can erode ossicles

### Clinical Features
- Foul discharge from ear
- Hearing loss (conductive)
- Pressure, pain, or numbness at ear

**Advanced**
- Dizziness
- Vertigo
- Facial muscle weakness

### Diagnosis
- Ear exam: white mass behind TM
- CT imaging and MRI assess the extent
