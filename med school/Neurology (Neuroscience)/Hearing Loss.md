#### Tinnitus
- Sound perceived in the absence of stimulus
- Causes can include:
	- Ontological conditions (e.g., cerumen impaction, otosclerosis, otitis media),
	- Sensorineural conditions (e.g., presbycusis, Meniere's disease),
	- Medications (e.g., NSAIDs, tetracyclines, quinine),
	- Metabolic disorders (e.g., hyperthyroidism, zinc deficiency),
	- Neurological conditions (e.g., vestibular schwannoma, multiple sclerosis),
	- Musculoskeletal issues (e.g., TMJ dysfunction),
	- Psychological factors (e.g., anxiety, depression)
	- Vascular conditions (e.g., glomus jugulare, arterio-venous malformations)
#### Noise-induced hearing loss
- Damage to hair cells in organ of Corti
#### Prebycusis
- Degenerative changes or death to hair cells and changes in auditory nerve
- Bilateral sensorineural loss
- 33% of those aged 65, 50% of those aged 75
#### Drug-induced Ototoxicity
- Aminoglycoside antibiotics (e.g. streptomycin, neomycin, gentamicin)
- Platinum-based chemotherapeutic agents (e.g. cisplatin, carboplatin)
- Loop diuretics (e.g. furosemide, bumetanide)
- High dose salicylates (aspirin)
- Others: Erectile dysfunction drugs, Quinine, Some organic solvents, vancomycin
