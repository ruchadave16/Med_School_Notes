>[!Warning] < 3.5 mM
>Serum potassium does NOT reflect body potassium since most is in cells

#### Causes
- GI loss
	- Severe diarrhea
	- Vomiting
		- low fluids so ↑ aldosterone so Na+ reabsorbed and K+ secreted
	- Laxative abuse
- ↑ renal excretion
	- K-losing [[thiazide diuretics]]
	- [[Loop Diuretics]]
	- [[Conn Syndrome|Primary hyperaldosteronism]]
	- Activation of RAAS 
	- Type 1 [[Renal Tubular Acidosis]] causes
	- Type 2 [[Renal Tubular Acidosis|RTA]] causes
	- Hereditary renal conditions
- Intracellular shift of K
	- [[Insulin]]
	- [[Alkalosis]]
	- [[Beta Agonist]]
- Severe burns
#### Physiology
- Low extracellular K+ causes 3Na+/2K+ pump and volt-gated K+ channels to be less active
- Re-polarizing currents ↓ 
- Plateau duration ↑ in [[Cardiac Ventricular Action Potential|cardiac action potential]]
#### Symptoms
- Afterdepolarizations -- arrhythmia
- Muscle cramps
- Spasms
#### Diagnosis
- Urine potassium
	- potassium < 20 mEq/L means GI loss
	- potassium > 20 mEq/L = renal loss
- Check pH 
- Inverted T waves or even a U wave

#### Treatment
- Oral repletion
	- IF patient is stable with potassium serum levels greater than ~2.5 mEq/L without EKG changes
	- Potassium chloride -- 20-40mEq at a time
		- 10mEq of potassium replaces 0.1 mEq/L
		- Repeat in 4 hrs
- IV
	- IF EKG changes, <2.5, cannot tolerate
	- **NEVER give a large push of IV potassium, as this will cause a cardiac arrest**
	- Peripheral venous: 10 mEq/hr
	- Central venous: 20 mEq/hr
- Check magnesium
	- Magnesium is a cofactor for the Na/K pump so give both
