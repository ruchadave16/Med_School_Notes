- Normally, K+ concentration maintained by renal excretion and cellular uptake through Na+/K+ pump

>[!Warning] $>$ 6–7 mM = medical emergency 
#### Causes
- Escape of intracellular K+
	- [[Metabolic acidosis]] (inorganic acid),
	- Tissue breakdown
	- Crushing injury [[rhabdomyolysis]]
	- Internal bleeding
	- [[Digitalis]] or [[Digoxin]] use
	- [[Tumor Lysis Syndrome]]
	- [[Beta Blockers|beta blocker]]
	- [[Hyperglycemia]]
	- [[Succinylcholine]]
	- Transfusion of expired blood 
	- [[Hemolysis]] -- Pseudohyperkalemia
- ↓ excretion of K+
	- [[Hypoaldosteronism]]
	- renal failure
	- [[ACE Inhibitors]]
	- [[spironolactone]]
#### Physiology
- Reduces equilibrium potential (normally K+ is found in high quantities intracellularly)
	- ↓ [[Cardiac Ventricular Action Potential|cardiac action potential]]
	- Stops Na+ channels from resetting from inactivated state -> closed but activateable
- This reduces *fast Na+ channels* conduction

#### Symptoms
- [[Arrhythmias]] 
- Muscle weakness, flaccid paralysis
- ↓ reflexes
#### Diagnosis
- Check creatinine and GFR for kidney injury
- Check CPK for muscle breakdown
- Check digoxin level
- Check pH
- The classic progression starts with tall, peaked T waves (↑ repolarization K+ current), which can evolve to a widened QRS and a "sine wave" pattern (slow propagation)

#### Treatment
- EKG changes
	- IV calcium gluconate or calcium chloride to stabilize the cardiac membrane.
- Unstable or K > 6.5 mEq/L (TEMPORARY)
	- [[Insulin]] and D50 to shift K in
	- [[Beta Agonist]]
	- If acidosis, [[sodium bicarbonate]]
- ↓ K+ in body
	- [[Loop Diuretics]] (only in pts with proper renal function)
	- Cation-Exchange Resins
	- Hemodialysis if life threatening or end stage renal function
