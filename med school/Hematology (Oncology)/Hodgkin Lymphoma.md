### Etiology
- ~30 years old
- **Most common** cancer in young adults and adolescents

### Pathophysiology
- Arises in a single node or chain of nodes, and spreads first to anatomically contiguous lymphoid tissue
- Presence of neoplastic giant cell (Reed-Sternberg cell) from germinal B cell centers
	- Release cytokines to attract reactive lymphocytes, macrophages, and granulocytes
	- Mononuclear variants
	- Lacunar cells -- delicate or folded nucleus and abundant pale cytoplasm
	- Lymphohistiocytic variants

		- Numerous eosinophils, macrophages and plasma cells

	- Lymphocyte-rich
		- Best prognosis

	- Lymphocyte-depleted
	
2) Nodular lymphocyte predominance
	- CD20+, CD15-, CD30-
	- L&H (popcorn) cells

### Clinical Features
- Painless lymphadenopathy
- Fever, night sweats, and weight loss

### Diagnosis
- Labs
- Imaging
- Criteria

### Treatment
- Chemotherapy
- Radiation therapy
- Immune checkpoint inhibitors
### Complications
- Short-term
- Long-term

### High-Yield Associations
- Stage I and II disease = cure rates of ~90%
