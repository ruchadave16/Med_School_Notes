### Etiology
- Primary causes
	- [[Community Acquired Pneumonia|CAP]] bacteria
	- [[Klebsiella Pneumonia]]
- Risk factors
	- Aspiration: Poor dental hygiene, malnutrition, alcohol abuse or intravenous drug abuse
	- Hematogenous
	- Inoculation
	- Transdiaphragmatic spread
	- Reactivation of [[Tuberculosis|TB]]

### Pathophysiology
- Collection of pus in pleural space
	- Positive Gram stain/culture with thick purulent fluid
- Infected end of parapneumonic effusion

1) Free flowing fluid
2) Fibrinopurulent -- Bacterial invasion, fibrin deposition
3) Organizing -- Thick fibrinous “peel” encases lung
	- ↓ expansion

### Diagnosis
- Labs
	- pH is low (<7.2), glucose is low (<60 mg/dl), protein high, high LDH, high WBC: high neutrophil usually suggests pyogenic bacterial infection
	- High lymphocytes may indicate tuberculosis or fungal infections.
- Imaging
	- Chest x-ray, ultrasound, CT
- Criteria

### Treatment
- Always requires drainage (tube thoracostomy ± fibrinolytics ± VATS) + antibiotics
- Surgical removal if 3)
