- 3 layers of membrane
	- Endothelial cell wall
	- The basement membrane
	- Type I pneumocyte

Dalton’s Law of Partial Pressures: pressure by 1 gas = total * fractional concentration
- Dry air: 160 mm Hg (760 * 0.21)
- Humidified tracheal: PTotal = 760 mm Hg – 47 mm Hg = 713 mm Hg (account for H2O) so PO2 = 713 * 0.21 = 150 mmHg

- Amount of gas depends on partial pressure, in equilibrium with partial pressure of gas in alveolar air after blood passes through lungs
- This difference in partial pressure causes blood to gain $O_2$ and lose $CO_2$

**Diffusion**
- Fick's law: *Vx = DL * DP*
	- V = volume of gas transferred per minute (mL / min)
	- DL = lung diffusing capacity (mL / min / mm Hg)
		- permeability of the alveolar-pulmonary capillary barrier 
		- proportional to the diffusion coefficient of the gas and surface area 
		-  inversely proportional to thickness of the barrier
	- DP = partial pressure difference of gas (mm Hg)
- Capacity of diffusion depends on → SA of [[alveoli]] and thickness of membrane

- Decrease in alveolar $P_{O2}$ relative to air is because of water vapor in air and also loss of $O_2$ since it leaves alveoli to load venous blood with $O_2$. 
	- Opposite is why $CO_2$ 
	- Should show **complete equilibrium** of $P_{O2}$ and $P_{CO2}$ between alveolar air and blood leaving lungs
	- In tissues, opposite movement
#### Alveolar Gas Equation
PAO2 = FIO2 (PB – PH2O) – (PaCO2/R)
- FIO2 is the fraction of inspired air that is O2
- PB is the barometric pressure 
- PH2O is the vapor pressure of H2O in the alveoli 
- PaCO2 is the arterial CO2 tension
- R is the respiratory quotient

PAO2 = 150 mm Hg – 1.25 (PaCO2)
#### Alveolar-Arterial O2 Content
- Difference between PAO2 and PaO2 should be < 15 mmHg
	- Gradient should be less than the patient’s age/4 + 4
- Important to determine cause of [[hypoxemia]]
