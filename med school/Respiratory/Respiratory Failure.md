1) [[Hypoxemia]]: PaO2 < 60 mmHg with normal or low PaCO2, gas exchange issue
- Shunt -- alveoli filled or collapsed 
	- [[ARDS]]
	- [[Pulmonary edema]]
	- Lobar [[pneumonia]]
	- [[Atelectasis]]
	- R to L shunts
- V/Q mismatch
	- [[Pulmonary Embolism|PE]]
	- [[COPD]]
	- Early Pneumonia
	- Interstitial lung disease
- Diffusion limit
	- [[Emphysema]]
	- Interstitial fibrosis
	
2) Hypercapnic: PaCO2 > 50 mmHg, ventilation issue
- **O2 can worsen this but still never hold O2 from hypoxic patient -- titrate to an SpO2 of ~88–92%**

Acute: pH is markedly deranged -- acute respiratory acidosis with hypercapnia because no renal compensation

Chronic: the kidney retains HCO3−, so pH is near-normal despite a high PaCO2 and high bicarbonate
