### Etiology
Direct
- [[Pneumonia]] / Respiratory viruses
- Aspiration
- Inhalation injury
- Drowning
- O2 toxicity

Indirect
- [[Sepsis]] -- MOST COMMON cause of ARDS
- Acute pancreatitis, lupus, near-drowning, toxic shock syndrome
- Trauma -> fat embolism
- Massive transfulsion
- Burns, [[DIC]], uremia
- Drug toxicity
### Pathophysiology
- Rapid onset respiratory failure -> **diffuse alveolar damage** 
- Damage starts by the alveolar walls -> pro inflammatory events
- Macrophages release cytokines and recruit [[neutrophils]]
	- These neutrophils release ROS, proteases and destroy endothelium
		-  Fills alveoli with protein rich fluid 
	- [[Type II Pneumocytes|type II pneumocyte]] damage: ↓ [[Pulmonary Surfactant|pulmonary surfactant]] so lungs collapse
	- **Hyaline membranes** form
- Alveoli affected are *Perfused but NOT ventilated* so V/Q decreases
	- Blood flows past and reaches L heart while being deoxygenated so R -> L shunt forms
	- O2 does NOT fix hypoxia
	- Widened A-a gradient
- Consolidated heavy lungs that become stiff
- Hypoxic vasoconstriciton means [[Pulmonary Hypertension|pulmonary hypertension]]

### Clinical Features
- Dyspnea within hours or days
- Diffuse crackles
- Cyanosis and arterial hypoxemia -> refractory to O2 therapy

### Diagnosis
- Labs
- Imaging
	- Bilateral opacities on chest X-ray/CT
	- Lung ultrasound -- confluence of B-lines
	- Echo to EXCLUDE cardiogenic pulmonary edema 
- Criteria
	- 1 week of known injury or new resp symptoms
	- Bilateral opacities on chest X-ray/CT, lung ultrasound
	- Edema that is non-cardiogenic -- echo
	- Mild: PaO2/FiO2 200–300 · Moderate: 100–200 · Severe: ≤ 100
### Treatment
- Ventilation
- Prone positioning reduces mortality
- Net-negative balance of fluids shortens time on the ventilator
- [[Nitric Oxide]] increases oxygenation but NOT mortality

### Complications
- 40-60% mortality rate
	- Ventilator induced lung injury
	- [[Pulmonary fibrosis]]
	- Pneumonia
	- 
- Development of interstitial fibrosis post-recovery
