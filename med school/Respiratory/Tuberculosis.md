### Etiology
- [[Mycobacterium tuberculosis]]
- ~22% of the global population is infected with tuberculosis (latent+ active)

### Pathophysiology
- Modifies phagosome (for [[Phagocytosis]]) so it can’t fuse with [[lysosome]]
- Allows it to proliferate inside [[Macrophage|macrophages]] and protect from antibodies

- First 3 weeks
	- [[Macrophage]] target
	- Mannose binding receptor
	- Surfactant A binds to M. tuberculosis and phagocytes through alveolar macrophage
	- Avoid degradation by endosome: ↓ acidification, no autophagosome formation
- [[Granuloma]] formation -- [[caseous necrosis]] surrounded by epithelioid cells
	- **Cord factor** induces TNF-a from macrophages
	- Survives in macrophages -> release of [[IL-12]]
	- ↓ respiratory burst so no killing of bacteria (NO)
	- Recruitment of healthy macrophages to site -- granuloma
	- [[Th1]] cells activated by IL-12 which further amplify macrophage recruitment
		- Secrete IFN-y
- Post 3 Weeks
	- *Ghon complex*: Granuloma + ipsilateral mediastinal lymphadenopathy, can spread to other organs
	- Usually ASYMPTOMATIC
- Generally goes dormant
	- Can have seeding in other organs

- **Secondary Tuberculosis**
	- Lung
	- Lymphadenitis -- scrofula
	- Meninges
	- Vertebral bodies  -- Pott Disease
	- Almost all organs
### Clinical Features
**Active**
- Malaise
- Anorexia
- Weight loss
- Fever (remergent, appears in afternoon and goes away)
- Night sweats
- Blood in cough 
- Pleuritic pain
- Can present as [[tuberculosis meningitis]]
### Diagnosis
- Sputum Smear on [[Ziehl-Neelsen Stain|acid fast stain]]
- PCR on sputum
	- Check [[rifampin]] resistance
- NAAT
- CSF has fungal / protozoal appearance
- Chest xray: upper lobe infiltrates, cavitations
### Treatment
**ACTIVE**
- RIPE x 2 months → RI x 4 months
	- [[Rifampin]] 
	- [[Isoniazid]]
	- [[Pyrazinamide]]
	- [[Ethambutol]]

**LATENT**
- Once weekly [[isoniazid]] and [[rifapentine]] for 12 weeks by directly observed therapy
- 4 months of daily [[Rifampin]]
- 3 months of daily isoniazid plus rifampin
- 6 months of daily INH along with vitamin B 6

**Drug Resistant**
- Second-line agents: [[fluoroquinolones]] (levofloxacin, moxifloxacin), [[aminoglycosides]] (amikacin), [[linezolid]], [[bedaquiline]], [[delamanid]]
- Higher risk of toxicity and failure

>[!Warning] Monitor LFTs, uric acid, vision (ethambutol), and peripheral neuropathy
### Complications
- [[Caseous Necrosis|Caseous necrosis]]
- [[Military TB]]
- [[Pott Disease]]
- Rasmussen aneurysm
- TB meningitis

### Prevention
- Bacillus Calmette–Guérin (BCG) vaccine:
	- Live attenuated M. bovis
	- Given in endemic countries to prevent severe forms (e.g., TB meningitis in children)
	- Not routinely used in the U.S.
- Infection control:
	- Isolation of active cases, contact tracing, and treatment of latent infections are essential to public health
- DOT (Directly Observed Therapy):
	- Used to ensure adherence and reduce resistance development
