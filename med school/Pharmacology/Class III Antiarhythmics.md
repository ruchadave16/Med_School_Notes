- [[Potassium Channel Blockers]]
#### Action
- Prolongs repolarization by blocking K+ channels (Phase 3)
- ↑ QT interval so lowers propagation of impulses

[[Amiodarone]] & [[Dronedarone]]: Affect all 4 Vaughan Williams classes
- Na⁺ channel blockade (Class I)
-  β-blocking (Class II)
- K⁺ channel blockade (Class III)
- Ca²⁺ channel blockade (Class IV)

[[Sotalol]]: Class II + Class III (β-blocker + K⁺ channel blocker)
#### Uses
- [[Amiodarone]]: Supraventricular and ventricular arrhythmias if structural heart disease
- [[Dofetilide]]: Afib or Atrial flutter, use in heart failure
- [[Dronedarone]]: Paroxysmal or persistent AF in patients in sinus rhythm; avoid in symptomatic heart failure
- [[Ibutilide]]: Pharmacologic cardioversion of atrial fibrillation/flutter
- [[Sotalol]]: Supraventricular and ventricular arrhythmias; avoid in reduced ejection fraction ([[HFrEF]])

#### Adverse Effects
- Boxed Warnings:
	- Life-threatening arrhythmias (especially TdP)
	- Pulmonary toxicity (Amiodarone)
	- Hepatotoxicity (Amiodarone)
	- Proarrhythmia (All agents)

- Other Important Effects:
	- Bradycardia, hypotension (esp. with IV Amiodarone)
	- Photosensitivity, blue-gray skin discoloration (Amiodarone)
	- Ocular toxicity (Amiodarone) → Optic neuritis, corneal deposits
	- Thyroid dysfunction (Amiodarone) → Hypo- or hyperthyroidism
	- Heart failure exacerbation → Dronedarone, sotalol (use with caution)
#### Contraindication
- All: Baseline QTc > 440–500 ms; history of Torsades de Pointes
- Amiodarone: Iodine allergy, bradycardia without pacemaker
- Dofetilide: Severe renal impairment (CrCl < 20 mL/min); many CYP3A4 interactions
- Dronedarone: Symptomatic heart failure, permanent AF, liver/lung toxicity from amiodarone
- Sotalol: Asthma, baseline low potassium, heart block without pacemaker
