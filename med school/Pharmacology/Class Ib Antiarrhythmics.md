- [[Sodium Channel Blockers]]
- **CORRECT metabolic abnormalities first**
#### Action
- Mild Na⁺ channel blockade, with shortened action potential duration
- Block fast sodium channels in phase 0 but rapidly dissociate 
	- Key Feature: Selective for ischemic or depolarized ventricular tissue
- Minimal effect on atrial or healthy ventricular tissue
#### Uses
- [[Lidocaine]]: Acute ventricular arrhythmias
	- Caution in pregnancy
- [[Mexiletine]]: Oral analog of lidocaine; used for chronic suppression of ventricular arrhythmias and symptomatic PVCs
	- Isolated case reports of safe use in pregnancy
#### Resistance

#### Dosing
- [[Lidocaine]] = IV only, used in emergency settings
- [[Mexiletine]] = oral, used for chronic ventricular arrhymias
- Monitor LFTs

#### Adverse Effects
- Proarrhythmia -- **↑ mortality in asymptomatic pt post MI**
- CNS toxicity -- mainly lidocaine
- Liver toxicity -- mexiletine, rare
- [[DRESS syndrome]]
- Blood dyscrasias -- rare in acutely ill pts (agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia)
- [[Heart failure]] exacerbation

#### Contraindication
- Heart blocks (2nd or 3rd degree) without a pacemaker
- Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome
- Adam-Stokes syndrome (sudden episodes of syncope due to heart block)
- Cardiogenic shock
