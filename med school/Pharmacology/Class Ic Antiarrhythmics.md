- [[Sodium Channel Blockers]]
#### Action
- Strong Na+ channel blocking during Phase 0 
- Slow conduction across all heart tissue
- Minimal effect on repolarization or action potential duration
#### Uses
- [[Flecainide]]: Conversion and maintenance of paroxysmal AF or SVT (e.g., AVNRT); PVC suppression
- [[Propafenone]]: Prevent recurrent paroxysmal AF, SVT, or ventricular arrhythmias
#### Resistance

#### Dosing
- Use with rate controlling agent: [[beta blockers]] or [[Non-dihydropyridines|Non-DHPs]]
	- Stops 1:1 AV conduction during atrial flutter

#### Adverse Effects
- Proarrhythmia -- ↑ mortality if ventricular arrhythmias and prior MI
- Vtach or Vfib in Afib/Aflutter patients 
	- **Coadminister with beta blockers**
- CNS effect -- caution with driving and operating machinery
- Hepatotoxicity -- Rare fulminant hepatitis and liver dysfunction
- [[Agranulocytosis]] -- Rare
- Exacerbation of heart failure
- [[Myasthenia Gravis]] worsening
- Male infertility (reversible) with propafenone

#### Contraindication
- Prior MI, heart failure, or structural heart disease
- Significant conduction blocks
- Cardiogenic shock, severe hypotension
- Brugada syndrome -- propafenone
- Severe obstructive lung disease
