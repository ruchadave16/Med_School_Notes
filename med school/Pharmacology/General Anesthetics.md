*Drugs that allow for a reversible change in consciousness*

**Goals**
- Unconsciousness
- [[Amnesia]]
- Analgesia
- ↓ Autonomic Reflexes
- Skeletal Muscle Relaxation

**Mechanism**
- [[GABA A Receptors|GABA A receptor]] -> [[Propofol]], [[etomidate]], [[barbiturates]], volatile anesthetics
	- Cl- (frequency or duration of opening) so hyperpolarization
- [[Glycine]] -> volatile anesthetics
	- ↑ inhibitory in spinal cord and brainstem
- [[NMDA Receptors]] Inhibition-> [[ketamine]], [[NO]], xenon
	- ↓ excitatory so ↓ Na and Ca2+ influx
- [[Nicotinic Receptors|Nicotinic receptor]] Inhibition
	- ↓ excitatory [[Ach]] activity
- K2P
	- ↑ K+ efflux so hyperpolarization
#### Steps
1) Analgesia
2) Excitement -- [[delirium]], agitation, hyperventilation, involuntary movements
3) Surgical Anesthesia -- reach the goals above
4) Medullary Depression -- resp. and CV depression and can cause death
#### Modern Practice: Balanced
- IV induction agent: [[Propofol]], [[thiopental]], [[etomidate]]
- [[Inhaled anesthetic]] to maintain state
- Analgesic adjunct: [[Opioids]]
- Muscle relaxant: [[Rocuronium]], [[succinylcholine]]
- Sedatives to reduce anxiety: [[Midazolam]], [[dexmedetomidine]]
- Anticholinergic: [[Atropine]], [[glycopyrrolate]]
#### Monitoring
- Resp. function: Oxygenation, ventilation, and airway patency 
- CV stability: BP, HR, tissue perfusion
- Neuromuscular function
- CNS activity: EEG
#### Effects
- Hemodynamic -> hypotension
- Respiratory effects
- Thermoregulatory control impairment
	- Need active warming measures
	- Postanesthesia Shivering 
- Postoperative Nausea and Vomiting (PONV)
	- [[Ondansetron]] (5-HT3 antagonist) used to prevent
